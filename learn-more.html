Why I built this

I did not set out to build a website. I was studying for the SAT and I wanted more grammar practice than I could find, so I started making my own.

I worked on it with my tutor. We wrote the questions ourselves — our own sentences, our own answer choices, our own explanations. To decide what was worth drilling, we went off the grammar skills the College Board publicly describes the test as covering, and we looked at SAT practice material available online to get a feel for how the questions are usually shaped. Nothing was copied from anywhere. Every question here is original.

For a while it was just a growing pile of questions that one person was using. Once there were a few hundred, it seemed a bit silly that they were sitting in a folder on my computer. So I turned it into a website and opened it up to other students.

It is still the thing I wanted when I was studying: a lot of grammar questions, and an explanation for every choice rather than just the right one. If you are preparing for the same test, it is yours now too.

What this is

A practice site for the grammar and writing section of the SAT, and nothing else. No reading, no math, no plans to add them.

The Writing section rewards a small, finite set of grammar rules. Learn the rules, drill them until they are automatic, and the section stops being a reading-speed test. Everything here is built around that one idea.

You can practice one rule at a time, pick a difficulty, or let it choose for you and go after whatever you’ve been getting wrong. If you keep missing the same thing, the per-rule tracking will tell you which one is actually costing you points rather than which one feels hardest — those are often not the same rule.

Tutors and teachers are welcome to use it too. Because every question explains all four choices, a student working alone still gets the reasoning someone would otherwise have to sit next to them and supply.

    How practice works

    Four steps, repeated.

    1

    Answer a mixed set

    Ten questions is enough to find your weak points.

    2

    Read the explanations

    Including the three you didn’t pick. That’s the part that moves a score.

    3

    Drill what you missed

    Adaptive practice targets your weakest rules at the right difficulty.

    4

    Watch mastery climb

    Per rule, weighted toward recent attempts, and held low until it means something.

    You choose the length and the difficulty before every set. Nothing starts until you’ve picked both, so a session is never a surprise.

    Timed practice

    A clock that matches real pacing, and an analysis afterwards of whether you were rushing or stalling.

    You can flag questions to revisit and see everything left blank before you submit, the way a real test interface works. Sessions survive a closed tab — leave in the middle of a set and it resumes where you stopped, with your answers intact.

    Personalized learning

    Adaptive practice tracks mastery per rule and keeps serving the ones you keep missing, at the difficulty you’re ready for.

    Mastery is not a raw percentage. It weighs your recent attempts more heavily than old ones, and stays low until you’ve answered enough questions on a rule for the number to mean anything — so a lucky run of three doesn’t read as mastery.

    Mistake tracking

    Missed questions come back automatically until you get them right, then stay gone.

    Beyond the review queue, the site looks for patterns across your mistakes: which rules cost you most, whether you fade late in a set, and whether you answer wrong questions faster than right ones — which usually means guessing rather than a knowledge gap.

    Progress analytics

    Analysis, not just numbers.

    The progress page shows accuracy over time, mastery by rule and by domain, and a breakdown of where your time goes. Every figure is computed from your own attempts; nothing is estimated or extrapolated from other users.

    Daily challenges

    The same set for everyone, every day.

    A short daily set drawn from the free question bank, identical for every student so the results are comparable. A five-minute habit that compounds.

    Achievements and streaks

    Badges, a streak calendar, and a leaderboard you can opt out of entirely.

    Streaks count days you practiced, not questions you got right, so a bad day doesn’t break one. The leaderboard is opt-in and shows usernames only.

    Question quality and originality

    This site contains no College Board material. Every sentence, every answer choice, and every explanation was written from scratch for this project, guided only by the published list of grammar skills the exam assesses.

    Duplicate prompts are blocked at the database level, and every question is reviewed against a checklist before publication: one defensible answer, four distinct choices, and a written reason for each wrong option.

    FAQ

    Do I need an account?

    To practice, yes — attempts have to be recorded against someone. The cheat sheets are readable without one.

    Is it free?

    The practice bank, the cheat sheets, timed mode, the review queue and progress tracking are free. There is a premium tier with additional material; nothing that is free today will move behind it.

    Do you cover reading and math?

    No. This is the grammar and writing section only, and there are no plans to expand.

    Are these real SAT questions?

    No, and deliberately so. Reproducing exam material would be a copyright breach. These are original questions written to test the same skills.

    What happens if I close the tab mid-session?

    Nothing is lost. Answers are saved as you go and the session resumes where you left it.

    Can I practice one specific rule?

    Yes. Every rule has its own practice set, reachable from the rules page or from the bottom of its cheat sheet.

    About the project

    One section of one exam, done properly rather than every section done adequately.

    The questions, the cheat sheets and the explanations are all original writing. If you find a question that is wrong, ambiguous, or has two defensible answers, there’s a report button on every one of them — that feedback genuinely gets read, and items do get rewritten because of it.

    Technically it’s a plain static site over a Postgres database. Answer keys live on the server rather than in the page, so you can’t read them out of the HTML — which would rather defeat the point.
